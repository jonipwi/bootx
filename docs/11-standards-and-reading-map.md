# Standards and Authoritative Reading Map

**Links verified:** 2026-07-12  
**Status:** starting map, not a complete systematic literature review or legal compliance analysis

## 1. Purpose

The BootX corpus contains valuable original values and metaphors, but professional research must compare them with external evidence and established governance frameworks. The sources below are authoritative starting points. Using this map does not establish compliance, certification, or scientific validity.

Before a real deployment, add applicable law, sector regulation, national guidance, technical standards, peer-reviewed human-factors research, and local community knowledge for each target jurisdiction and population.

## 2. Core sources

### AI risk management

- [NIST Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10) — voluntary lifecycle-oriented framework for managing AI risks. Use its Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage structure to cross-check BootX governance and assurance.
- [NIST Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) — generative-AI risk profile. Use it when the companion includes a generative model; do not assume a general AI risk register covers model-specific hazards.
- [NIST AI Resource Center](https://airc.nist.gov/) — implementation resources and playbook material. Check for revisions before each formal review because risk guidance evolves.

### Human rights and ethics

- [UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence) — adopted in 2021 and organized around human rights, dignity, inclusion, oversight, accountability, sustainability, and lifecycle governance.
- [UNESCO Ethical Impact Assessment](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ethical-impact-assessment-tool-recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence) — practical impact-assessment instrument. Compare it with the BootX rights assessment and decision-log templates.
- [OECD AI Principles](https://oecd.ai/en/principles) — principles for trustworthy AI, initially adopted in 2019 and updated in 2024. Use for interoperability and public-policy comparison, not as a replacement for local obligations.

### Children and AI companions

- [UNICEF Guidance on AI and Children, version 3.0](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children) — published in December 2025 and explicitly updated for current AI and companion risks. Child-facing BootX work should undergo a dedicated rights and safeguarding review against this guidance before research or deployment.

### Privacy engineering

- [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework) — voluntary enterprise privacy-risk framework. The NIST site currently presents version 1.0 material and version 1.1 development material; record the exact version used in every BootX assessment.

## 3. BootX crosswalk

| BootX handbook area | External material to consult first | Research question |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation and scope | UNESCO Recommendation; OECD Principles | Are purpose, affected rights, and lifecycle responsibilities complete? |
| Logic and evidence | NIST AI RMF Measure; domain research methods | Are validity, reliability, calibration, and uncertainty appropriate to the use? |
| Mathematics | NIST AI RMF; peer-reviewed statistics and human-factors work | Do measures reflect real constructs and unequal error costs? |
| Ethics and governance | UNESCO Recommendation and Ethical Impact Assessment | Are dignity, oversight, participation, sustainability, and remedy operational? |
| AI DNA | all sources above | Which requirements overlap, conflict, or remain uniquely hypothetical? |
| Architecture and security | NIST GenAI Profile; current security standards for the platform | Are model, tool, data, supply-chain, and recovery risks controlled? |
| Companion memory | NIST Privacy Framework; applicable data-protection law | Can collection be minimized and user rights work technically? |
| Child or family use | UNICEF Guidance | Are best interests, development, inclusion, privacy, and safeguarding demonstrated? |
| Field evaluation | domain ethics, accessibility, and research guidance | Is participation legitimate and is evidence transferable? |

## 4. Literature-review protocol

For every proposed capability:

1. define the question using population, intervention, comparator, outcome, context, and time;
2. search multiple scholarly and standards databases with recorded strings and dates;
3. prioritize primary research and authoritative sources;
4. record inclusion, exclusion, quality appraisal, conflicts, and funding;
5. extract benefits, harms, populations, measures, and uncertainty;
6. identify where evidence does not transfer to the intended users or environment;
7. publish a source table and update date;
8. repeat when the model, context, law, or material evidence changes.

AI can assist discovery and extraction, but a qualified human remains responsible for source verification and interpretation.

## 5. Evidence gap register

The current handbook still requires systematic review in at least these areas:

- scam-warning effectiveness and warning fatigue;
- calibrated reliance on AI advice;
- emotional attachment and dependency in companion systems;
- memory, privacy, and user-controlled personalization;
- accessibility across disability, language, literacy, age, and culture;
- local-first versus cloud security and lifecycle cost;
- protective interventions in coercive relationships;
- environmental impacts of model and device choices;
- long-term effects on independent judgment and human relationships;
- validity and reliability of every proposed AI DNA indicator.

Until those reviews and studies are complete, BootX claims in these areas remain hypotheses or design commitments rather than established outcomes.

## 6. Citation rule for future BootX work

Every empirical statement that materially affects design or policy should link to a source that directly supports it. Include authoring body, title, version, publication date, access date for evolving web material, and the exact claim supported. Clearly label analogy, opinion, scenario, and illustrative mathematics.
