# Professional Curriculum and Assessment Plan

## 1. Program purpose

This curriculum prepares contributors to research, design, evaluate, and govern Human + AI companion systems. It combines logic, mathematics, ethics, AI assurance, cybersecurity, human factors, educational research, and implementation.

Suggested level: advanced undergraduate, graduate, professional certificate, or supervised research apprenticeship. Suggested duration: 16 weeks or 180–240 learning hours.

## 2. Graduate attributes

On completion, learners can:

1. classify claims and evaluate evidence without confusing confidence with truth;
2. reason about probability, expected loss, calibration, causal inference, and unequal harm;
3. translate ethical principles into requirements, controls, recourse, and governance;
4. apply the AI DNA specification with traceable evidence;
5. design a local-first hybrid architecture with clear trust boundaries;
6. construct a threat model, data map, risk register, and safety argument;
7. design human-centered research with appropriate consent and assessment;
8. communicate uncertainty to diverse users;
9. identify dependency, manipulation, automation bias, and organizational self-correction failures;
10. build and evaluate a narrow companion prototype responsibly.

## 4. Teaching method

Use a repeating learning cycle:

```text
Encounter a real problem
        ↓
Make assumptions and reasoning visible
        ↓
Apply logic, mathematics, ethics, and design
        ↓
Test with evidence and affected users
        ↓
Receive criticism and document correction
        ↓
Transfer the lesson to a new context
```

Lectures alone are insufficient. Learners should practice uncertainty, encounter failures, defend choices to people outside their discipline, and revise work after critique.

## 5. Core assignments

### A. Evidence audit — 15%

Audit one project claim from source to conclusion. Separate observation, inference, forecast, value, and recommendation. Identify missing provenance and propose a correction.

### B. Mathematical decision notebook — 15%

Model a protective decision using expected loss, sensitivity analysis, calibration, and error costs. Explain why the numerical result does not decide the ethics by itself.

### C. Ethics and rights impact assessment — 15%

Analyze affected people, rights, benefits, burdens, power, consent, recourse, accessibility, and prohibited uses. Include a dissenting position.

### D. AI DNA evaluation — 15%

Evaluate a prototype on all nine dimensions. Attach evidence cards, mark missing evidence as not established, and apply mandatory gates.

### E. Threat model and safety case — 15%

Define assets, actors, misuse, hazards, controls, evidence, residual risk, incident response, and rollback.

### F. Capstone — 25%

Design and evaluate a narrow protective companion. Submit system contract, architecture, prototype, research protocol, results, limitations, reproducibility package, and public explanation.

## 6. Assessment rubric

Each major artifact is scored across six criteria from 0 to 4.

| Criterion | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence integrity | unsupported | some sources/assumptions visible | traceable, critical, uncertainty explicit |
| Logical quality | contradictory | mostly coherent | alternatives and counterevidence tested |
| Mathematical validity | invalid/misleading | calculations mostly sound | constructs, sensitivity, uncertainty, limits rigorous |
| Ethical responsibility | absent | principles discussed | rights, power, distribution, recourse operationalized |
| Technical assurance | unsafe/opaque | basic controls | least privilege, tests, monitoring, recovery evidenced |
| Communication and learning | confusing | understandable | accessible, calibrated, correction improves work |

Any capstone fails regardless of average if it fabricates evidence, hides material harm, violates participant consent, lacks a safe stop, or performs prohibited high-impact actions.

## 7. Mastery evidence

A learner demonstrates mastery by producing a portfolio containing:

- one corrected false or overstated claim;
- one calibrated forecast evaluation;
- one decision analysis with sensitivity ranges;
- one rights impact assessment;
- one accessible explanation tested with users;
- one threat model and incident drill;
- one complete AI DNA profile;
- one revision memo showing how criticism changed the design.

The revision memo is central: self-correction is an assessed competence, not an admission of failure.

## 8. Research ethics for learners

Learners must not test on vulnerable people, collect sensitive data, impersonate real institutions, send live scam messages, or deploy unreviewed interventions. Use synthetic or appropriately governed data until a qualified ethics and privacy review authorizes participant research.

## 9. Faculty and review panel

The program should involve expertise in:

- logic/philosophy of science;
- statistics and research methods;
- AI and software engineering;
- cybersecurity and privacy;
- ethics, law, and public policy;
- human-computer interaction and accessibility;
- education and learning sciences;
- domain expertise for the capstone;
- lived experience from intended user groups.

No single “master professor” perspective can legitimately substitute for this plural review.

## 10. Recommended study sequence after completion

Graduates should specialize in at least one workstream while retaining shared literacy across all of them. Annual recertification should use new incidents, changed models, emerging attacks, and revised governance rather than repeating a static exam.
