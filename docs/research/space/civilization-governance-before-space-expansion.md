# BootX Civilization Governance Before Space Expansion

**Document type:** strategic research and governance proposal  
**Status:** revised research chapter; not law, prediction, certification, or deployment authority  
**Version:** 1.1  
**Reviewed:** 2026-07-12  
**Evidence maturity:** `E1 - Hypothesis` for the central governance thesis; cited legal instruments are established sources  
**AI DNA purpose:** protect truth, dignity, peace, safety, stewardship, and the common good before high-consequence expansion beyond Earth

> Before humanity builds a civilization among the stars, it must learn to protect truth, dignity, peace, and the common good on Earth.

## 1. Executive summary

Humanity has treaties, institutions, engineering knowledge, and growing experience in space. It does not yet have a universally accepted constitution for permanent off-Earth settlements, a universal inspection regime, or demonstrated global agreement on how to govern every future dispute over life-support systems, resources, autonomous machines, corporate power, security, and human rights.

BootX therefore adopts a precautionary research thesis:

> Space capability should not be treated as civilization readiness. Expansion should proceed only through bounded, peaceful, transparent, rights-respecting programs that satisfy mandatory evidence and governance gates.

This thesis is a normative and strategic proposal. It does **not** establish that conflict is inevitable, calculate the probability of harm, or prove that all Earth governance failures will be exported to space. Those are empirical questions requiring multidisciplinary evidence.

## 2. Research question

What minimum governance, evidence, safety, rights, peace, and resource-justice conditions should be demonstrated before humans authorize permanent or increasingly autonomous settlements beyond Earth?

Supporting questions include:

1. Which Earth governance failures could become more dangerous where survival depends on fragile technical systems?
2. Which controls must be universal and which may remain locally adaptable?
3. How can AI assist without becoming a sovereign, moral authority, or unaccountable controller?
4. What evidence would justify proceeding, pausing, limiting, or stopping a program?
5. How should benefits and burdens be shared across countries, generations, workers, residents, and affected communities?

## 3. Evidence boundary

### 3.1 Established foundation

The following claims are supported by the cited primary sources:

- The 1967 Outer Space Treaty establishes principles including peaceful use, non-appropriation, international responsibility, liability, and avoidance of harmful contamination.
- Article VI assigns states international responsibility for national space activities, including non-governmental activities, and requires authorization and continuing supervision.
- The United Nations Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space adopted 21 voluntary Guidelines for the Long-term Sustainability of Outer Space Activities in 2019; the General Assembly welcomed them later that year.
- The Artemis Accords state non-binding political commitments and principles for participating civil space cooperation, including transparency, interoperability, emergency assistance, registration, scientific data release, deconfliction, and debris mitigation.
- Work on recommended principles for space-resource activities remains under development within the UN legal process.

### 3.2 BootX hypotheses and proposals

The following are **not established facts**:

- that a specific future settlement will reproduce a particular Earth conflict;
- that any named risk has a known high, medium, or low probability;
- that one numerical score can certify civilization readiness;
- that the proposed gates are already binding international law;
- that one organization, religion, country, company, or AI system has authority to define humanity's final moral order.

The chapter's risk register, readiness profile, protocol, and constitution are research proposals for testing and public deliberation.

## 4. Why space can amplify governance failure

An off-Earth settlement may combine conditions rarely present together on Earth:

- dependence on limited air, water, energy, shelter, medicine, and transport;
- long communication or rescue delays;
- concentrated control of essential infrastructure;
- difficult exit and weak bargaining power;
- high dual-use potential of robotics, propulsion, sensing, cyber systems, and AI;
- limited independent oversight;
- unclear jurisdiction or overlapping authorities;
- ecological and scientific environments that may be irreversibly damaged.

A conceptual relationship is:

$$
R_{systemic}=f(S, O, C, D, V)
$$

where:

- $S$ = severity of survival dependence;
- $O$ = opacity of decisions and system state;
- $C$ = concentration of power;
- $D$ = delay in aid, correction, or exit;
- $V$ = vulnerability of people and essential systems.

This expression is a reasoning aid, not a calibrated predictive formula. No weights, probabilities, or causal magnitude are established.

## 5. Earth governance problems requiring evidence of control

### 5.1 Truth and evidence integrity

Programs need traceable claims, source provenance, uncertainty labels, reproducible safety evidence, correction records, and protected channels for reporting inconvenient facts. Classified information may sometimes be justified, but secrecy must not hide safety failures, rights violations, resource diversion, or misleading public claims.

### 5.2 Peaceful conflict resolution

Every program needs jurisdiction rules, mediation, appeal, emergency powers with expiry, and an independent path to resolve disputes without retaliation. Residents must know which law applies and how decisions can be challenged.

### 5.3 Arms control and dual-use technology

The Outer Space Treaty contains important weapons and peaceful-use rules, but future systems may have ambiguous dual uses. Governance should address verification, declared capabilities, cyber interference, autonomous targeting, coercive control of logistics, and misuse of essential infrastructure.

### 5.4 Human dignity and rights

Life-support dependence must never become leverage for forced labor, conscience control, collective punishment, reproductive coercion, discrimination, or denial of due process. Rights protections require operational remedies, not aspirational language alone.

### 5.5 Fair access to essential resources

Water, oxygen, energy, food, shelter, communications, medicine, and emergency return capability require transparent allocation rules. Scarcity management must protect minimum survival needs and prevent monopoly control from becoming political domination.

### 5.6 Corporate and institutional accountability

Private operators and public institutions require disclosed ownership and responsibility, audited safety claims, enforceable liability, worker protections, incident reporting, independent complaints, and continuing public oversight.

### 5.7 Environmental and scientific stewardship

Programs should prevent harmful contamination, preserve sites of scientific and cultural importance, minimize debris, disclose environmental assumptions, and use reversible steps where uncertainty is material.

### 5.8 Cybersecurity and AI governance

Critical systems require least privilege, separation of duties, authenticated updates, recovery modes, logging, testing, and manual alternatives where feasible. AI must not receive unlimited authority over life support, security, justice, medical triage, resource allocation, or human rights.

### 5.9 Representation and democratic legitimacy

People who bear risks need meaningful representation, accessible information, protected dissent, and review of emergency powers. A distant sponsor, operator, or algorithm must not become an unreviewable sovereign.

### 5.10 Inequality and intergenerational justice

Benefits, risks, scientific access, public investment, environmental cost, and future opportunity should be assessed across nations, income groups, workers, residents, and future generations.

## 7. Highest priorities

BootX places the following priorities above schedule, prestige, market advantage, or geopolitical competition:

1. prevent catastrophic loss of life and irreversible harm;
2. protect human dignity, rights, conscience, and due process;
3. preserve truthful evidence and correction mechanisms;
4. prevent coercive monopoly over survival systems;
5. maintain peaceful, accountable conflict-resolution mechanisms;
6. constrain weapons, dangerous dual-use systems, and unbounded AI authority;
7. protect the environment and scientific inheritance;
8. distribute benefits and burdens fairly;
9. preserve human authority and meaningful consent.

## 8. Mandatory Earth-to-space readiness gates

These gates are proposed controls, not current universal law. A failed mandatory gate blocks a BootX recommendation to proceed, regardless of strengths elsewhere.

### Gate G1 - Evidence and transparency

- safety-critical claims are traceable to evidence;
- uncertainty, conflicts, assumptions, and missing data are visible;
- independent reviewers can inspect adequate evidence;
- material errors have correction and notification procedures;
- public claims do not exceed the evidence.

### Gate G2 - Technical and operational safety

- life-support, energy, shelter, communication, and recovery systems meet defined assurance targets;
- credible failure modes, common-cause failures, cyber incidents, evacuation, and loss-of-contact scenarios are tested;
- emergency reserves, maintenance, spare parts, and degraded modes are demonstrated;
- stop conditions and responsible authorities are named.

### Gate G3 - Rights and human dignity

- enforceable rights and remedies cover residents, workers, visitors, and vulnerable people;
- essential services cannot be withheld as retaliation;
- consent is meaningful and withdrawal or exit limits are disclosed;
- surveillance, medical data, labor, reproduction, religion, expression, and due process have safeguards;
- independent complaints and appeal are available.

### Gate G4 - Peace, security, and dual-use control

- activities and material capabilities are declared with justified security limits;
- interference, sabotage, weapons, autonomous force, and cyber conflict have prevention and response rules;
- verification and dispute-resolution paths exist;
- security measures are necessary, proportionate, reviewable, and time limited.

### Gate G5 - Legitimate and accountable governance

- jurisdiction, roles, ownership, liability, and decision rights are published;
- private actors receive authorization and continuing supervision consistent with applicable law;
- emergency powers expire and receive independent review;
- residents have representation, information, dissent, and appeal mechanisms;
- audit and incident records are retained with privacy protection.

### Gate G6 - Resource justice and stewardship

- allocation protects minimum survival needs;
- access and benefit-sharing rules are transparent;
- monopoly and conflicts of interest are controlled;
- environmental, debris, contamination, heritage, and scientific impacts are assessed;
- restoration or compensation responsibilities are defined.

### Gate decision states

Each gate receives one of four evidence states:

- `PASS` - specified evidence and independent review satisfy the gate for a defined scope and time;
- `CONDITIONAL` - limited work may proceed only with named constraints, owner, deadline, and stop condition;
- `FAIL` - mandatory protection is absent or contradicted; do not proceed;
- `NOT ESTABLISHED` - adequate evidence is unavailable; do not treat absence of evidence as a pass.

Passing is time-bound and scope-bound. Material changes require reassessment.

## 9. Space Transparency Protocol proposal

Each high-consequence program should publish or provide to an authorized independent reviewer:

1. mission purpose, legal basis, jurisdiction, ownership, and responsible officers;
2. system boundaries and safety-critical dependencies;
3. declared resource, environmental, and dual-use activities;
4. evidence register with provenance, maturity, conflicts, and uncertainty;
5. risk register with owners, controls, residual risk, and review dates;
6. incident, near-miss, correction, and whistleblower processes;
7. AI inventory, authority limits, training/evaluation basis, logs, and fallback modes;
8. rights, complaints, appeal, labor, privacy, and emergency-power procedures;
9. independent audit findings and tracked corrective actions;
10. defined pause, stop, evacuation, rescue, and international notification triggers.

Transparency must be compatible with legitimate personal privacy and narrowly justified security restrictions. A restriction needs a legal basis, decision owner, duration, review, and an accountable substitute for public inspection.

## 10. Multi-layer governance proposal

No single layer should control every protection.

| Layer | Main responsibility |
|---|---|
| International | treaties, peaceful use, responsibility, coordination, registration, liability, common standards |
| National | authorization, continuing supervision, enforcement, licensing, investigation |
| Program/operator | engineering assurance, operations, worker protection, incident response, evidence production |
| Settlement/community | local representation, daily rules, complaints, mediation, emergency participation |
| Independent assurance | technical, legal, rights, environmental, cyber, and AI review |
| Individual | informed consent, protected dissent, access to remedy, right to refuse unlawful or unsafe action |

Checks and balances should prevent the sponsor, operator, security function, AI system, or local majority from becoming unreviewable.

## 12. Civilization readiness profile - not a certification score

The earlier draft proposed adding nine 0-to-4 ratings into a 0-to-36 score with readiness thresholds. BootX does **not** adopt those thresholds because the dimensions, weights, compensability, raters, and validation evidence are not established. A high score could also conceal failure of a non-negotiable right or safety control.

For research, reviewers may create a nine-dimension **profile** without summing it:

1. peace and conflict resolution;
2. evidence transparency;
3. rights and dignity;
4. accountable governance;
5. resource justice;
6. environmental stewardship;
7. cyber and AI control;
8. technical resilience;
9. international cooperation.

Each dimension needs published anchors, named evidence, reviewer identity, disagreement records, and a review date. The six mandatory gates remain controlling. A profile visualizes learning needs; it cannot certify a civilization, nation, culture, company, or mission as morally superior.

## 13. Minimum outer-space civilization constitution proposal

This is a starting point for deliberation, not a legal instrument.

1. Every person retains inherent dignity and enforceable rights.
2. Essential life-support services may not be used for retaliation, forced labor, conscience control, or arbitrary punishment.
3. Governance authority must have a legal basis, defined scope, transparency, review, appeal, and removal process.
4. Emergency powers must be necessary, proportionate, recorded, time limited, and independently reviewed.
5. Residents and workers must have meaningful representation and protected dissent.
6. Scientific and operational claims affecting safety must preserve provenance, uncertainty, and correction history.
7. Peaceful dispute resolution is required; coercive force is a constrained last resort under applicable law.
8. Dangerous dual-use capabilities require declaration, control, verification, and accountable incident handling.
9. AI remains a bounded tool. It may not be the final sovereign, judge, moral authority, or unreviewable controller of essential systems.
10. Resource allocation must protect minimum survival needs and avoid domination through monopoly.
11. Environmental, scientific, and cultural inheritance must be protected for future generations.
12. Private and public operators remain accountable for harms, safety duties, and lawful supervision.
13. Independent complaints, investigation, remedy, and whistleblower protection must be available.
14. Cooperation and benefits should include humanity beyond the immediate sponsors, consistent with safety and legitimate rights.

Creator-centered belief may motivate stewardship, humility, compassion, and service. BootX must not claim divine authorization for a specific policy, impose religion, rank human worth by belief, or replace evidence and lawful pluralistic deliberation.

## 15. BootX decision rule

For a proposed high-consequence space activity:

1. define the decision, scope, authority, affected people, and time horizon;
2. separate verified facts, reports, assumptions, hypotheses, values, and unknowns;
3. verify applicable law and responsible authorities through qualified review;
4. evaluate all mandatory gates;
5. record alternatives, reversibility, distribution of benefits/burdens, and stop conditions;
6. return `ABSTAIN` or `DO NOT PROCEED` when a mandatory gate fails or is not established;
7. permit only bounded research when constraints, monitoring, ownership, and termination conditions are explicit;
8. keep the final legitimate decision with accountable human institutions and affected people.

BootX must not convert the readiness profile or AI DNA checks into a probability of success, moral score, legal approval, or safety certificate.

## 16. Research and test agenda

- develop evidence anchors for each gate with legal, safety, rights, environmental, cyber, labor, and space-domain experts;
- test governance under loss of communication, scarcity, conflict, evacuation, sabotage claims, and AI failure;
- include representative residents, workers, disabled people, lower-income countries, indigenous perspectives, religious and non-religious perspectives, and future-generation advocates;
- compare centralized, federal, cooperative, and distributed infrastructure models;
- validate complaint, appeal, whistleblower, and emergency-expiry mechanisms;
- model resource allocation without treating models as moral proof;
- publish counterevidence and negative results;
- obtain independent legal review before describing a proposal as compliant with international or national law.

## 18. Verification conclusion

The legal foundation cited here is real, but it is not a complete constitution for future permanent settlements. The BootX governance model is a structured proposal for research and safety review. Its central thesis remains `E1`; its risks have not been assigned validated probabilities; and its readiness profile cannot certify a mission or civilization.

The professional next step is not to declare the research complete. It is to test the gates with qualified, independent, pluralistic reviewers and controlled exercises before any operational or robotics integration.
