# Foundation and Scope

This research foundation implements the higher-level commitments in the [BootX Charter](../../CHARTER.md). Decision authority and amendments follow [BootX Governance](../../GOVERNANCE.md), while long-term stewardship follows the [Succession and Continuity Plan](../../SUCCESSION.md).

## 1. Research problem

AI capability is advancing faster than many people and institutions can evaluate its outputs, protect personal data, or recover from automated mistakes. At the same time, digital fraud, manipulated media, information overload, and unequal access to expertise weaken human decision-making.

BootX proposes a Human + AI companion as a protective and educational layer. The companion should translate complexity, identify risk, preserve memory responsibly, and help a person reflect before acting. The distinctive thesis is that intelligence must be joined to trust, humility, autonomy, and care.

## 2. Project thesis

The project rests on five linked propositions:

1. Capability without correction becomes fragile and potentially dangerous.
2. Trust is produced by verifiable conduct over time, not by personality or claims of benevolence.
3. A local-first, hybrid architecture can combine personal control with carefully bounded shared intelligence.
4. The companion should increase a person's capacity to judge and act, not make the person dependent on it.
5. Protection of humanity requires measurable safeguards at personal, family, community, and institutional scales.

These are working propositions. Each must remain open to criticism and empirical revision.

## 3. Meaning of “AI companion”

An AI companion is a user-accountable software system that provides bounded assistance across four functions:

- **Understand:** explain information, provenance, uncertainty, and alternatives.
- **Protect:** detect possible fraud, manipulation, security hazards, and urgent risks.
- **Remember:** store user-approved context with provenance, purpose limits, retention rules, and deletion controls.
- **Learn together:** help the user reflect on decisions, review outcomes, and improve future judgment.

Companionship is a service relationship, not evidence that the system is conscious, a person, a moral patient, a family member, or an infallible authority. The system must not encourage the user to confuse simulation of empathy with human reciprocity.

## 4. Scope boundaries

### In scope

- plain-language security and scam explanations;
- source and uncertainty display;
- consent-based personal memory;
- decision support for low- and moderate-impact choices;
- learning exercises and reflective review;
- trusted-contact escalation selected by the user;
- offline or degraded-mode protective functions;
- evaluation of trustworthiness, accessibility, and human agency.

### Out of scope for the initial program

- autonomous use of force or coercion;
- diagnosis, treatment, or emergency dispatch without qualified oversight;
- legal, financial, employment, insurance, or eligibility decisions;
- covert surveillance or behavioral persuasion;
- autonomous transfer of money, credentials, property, or legal consent;
- claiming certainty, consciousness, spiritual authority, or moral perfection;
- self-preservation objectives that override the user or authorized operators;
- a universal numerical “human value” score.

## 5. System levels

BootX currently combines two ideas that should be kept conceptually distinct:

| Level | Purpose | Assurance focus |
|---|---|---|
| Boot and operating-system foundation | Start hardware, isolate components, provide device and storage services | correctness, secure startup, memory safety, update recovery |
| Companion service | Explain, protect, remember, and assist | truthfulness, privacy, agency, safety, human factors |
| Trust network | Share narrowly scoped warnings and verification signals | provenance, federation, abuse resistance, governance |
| Research and governance program | Define measures, review evidence, authorize trials | independence, ethics, reproducibility, accountability |

A bootable image is not evidence of a trustworthy AI companion. Conversely, a safe companion prototype need not initially be embedded in a new operating system. Keeping these layers separate enables testing and prevents branding from substituting for assurance.

## 6. Theory of change

The proposed causal chain is:

```text
Transparent evidence + understandable explanations + bounded protective tools
                              ↓
Better comprehension + earlier risk recognition + meaningful user control
                              ↓
Safer decisions + increased learning + reduced manipulation and preventable loss
                              ↓
More resilient people, families, communities, and institutions
```

Every arrow is an empirical hypothesis. For example, more warnings can create fatigue; more explanation can overload users; more memory can create privacy risk; and protective automation can reduce skill. Evaluation must therefore test both benefit and unintended effects.

## 7. Educational R&D model

The program follows an iterative design-research cycle:

1. **Analyze:** identify learners, users, contexts, needs, risks, and prior evidence.
2. **Design:** define outcomes, constraints, interfaces, assessment, and failure handling.
3. **Develop:** build the smallest reversible prototype with instrumentation and documentation.
4. **Evaluate:** test usability, learning, safety, security, calibration, and equity.
5. **Reflect:** conduct incident and outcome reviews; compare predictions with results.
6. **Revise:** update claims, design, policy, and curriculum under version control.

This combines educational design with engineering assurance. A feature is not complete merely because it works technically; users must understand it sufficiently, outcomes must be measured, and known risks must be controlled.

## 8. Research questions

The core questions are:

- Does a plain-language companion improve detection of scams without unacceptable false alarms?
- Does it improve user calibration—confidence that tracks actual correctness?
- Can local-first memory provide useful continuity without excessive collection or retention?
- Which explanations increase understanding rather than compliance?
- How does the design affect children, older adults, people with disabilities, low-literacy users, and people under stress?
- Does repeated use increase independent skill or create dependency?
- Which decisions must always require qualified human review?
- Can governance detect and correct “Pharaoh Pattern” failures such as suppressed warnings, loyalty bias, and unchallengeable leadership?

## 9. Non-negotiable release principle

> The burden of proof grows with the scale, irreversibility, vulnerability, and consequence of the proposed use.

Low-impact prototypes may proceed with ordinary testing. High-impact uses require stronger evidence, independent review, secure operations, explicit authority, appeal, monitoring, and a demonstrated ability to stop the system safely.
